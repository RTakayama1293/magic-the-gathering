# Pioneer's February 2026 metagame: Greasefang and Cori-Steel Cutter rule

**Greasefang combo variants and prowess strategies collectively dominate Pioneer in late February 2026, accounting for over half the competitive metagame.** The format sits in a tense equilibrium where Cori-Steel Cutter—already banned in Standard—warps deck construction, Greasefang combo has surged to unprecedented levels, and the community watches closely for whether Wizards will intervene at the next ban window. For Soulflayer pilots, the news is grim: elevated graveyard hate and heavy main-deck interaction make this one of the worst metagames for the archetype in recent memory. Meanwhile, the TMNT Universes Beyond set drops March 6 with full Pioneer legality and several cards poised to shake things up.

---

## The Tier 1 gauntlet: four decks own 55% of the field

The current Pioneer metagame, per MTGGoldfish data through February 26, 2026, is concentrated around four dominant archetypes:

**Mono-Red Prowess (19.0%, ~200 decks)** holds the single largest meta share. The deck evolved from Rakdos Prowess after players realized **Sunspine Lynx** brutally punishes the non-basic-heavy mana bases of two-color decks, making mono-red the superior shell. Screaming Nemesis shuts down lifegain strategies, while Emberheart Challenger, Soul-Scar Mage, and Urabrask's Forge provide staying power beyond raw speed. The deck won back-to-back Challenges on February 8 and 22.

**Abzan Greasefang (16.8%, ~177 decks)** has surged to become the second most-played archetype—and when combined with its sister variant, the most dominant deck family in the format. The combo dumps Parhelion II into the graveyard and reanimates it with Greasefang, Okiba Boss for a potential turn-two kill producing **13 power of flying attackers**. Formidable Speaker from Lorwyn Eclipsed pushed the Abzan build ahead of the older Orzhov version by adding consistency to the combo assembly.

**Izzet Prowess (10.0%, ~105 decks)** is the deck built around the format's most controversial card. **Cori-Steel Cutter**, a two-mana equipment from Tarkir: Dragonstorm, creates a 1/1 Monk token with prowess whenever you cast your second spell each turn. In practice, this generates 2/2 or larger threats every single turn in a spellslinger shell. The card was banned in Standard after 42.3% of the Pro Tour Final Fantasy field registered Izzet Prowess; at the European Regional Championship, the deck posted a **56% win rate**—the highest of any archetype. Three Izzet Prowess decks made the Top 8 of the February 24 Challenge.

**Orzhov Greasefang (9.6%, ~101 decks)** pairs the Greasefang combo with Monument to Endurance as a backup win condition, making it more resilient to graveyard hate than pure combo builds. This variant posted a perfect **9-0 record** winning the February 21 Challenge. Combined, the two Greasefang variants represent **26.4% of the Pioneer metagame**—the largest deck family by a wide margin.

## Tier 2 features control, company, and Phoenix

The second tier contains five archetypes ranging from 5–8% meta share, each occupying distinct strategic niches:

**Azorius Control (8.0%)** has adapted specifically to fight prowess, running main-deck High Noon and Temporary Lockdown alongside Teferi, Hero of Dominaria and the new Consult the Star Charts from Lorwyn Eclipsed. **Selesnya Company (6.3%)** rides Badgermole Cub and Collected Company into a hatebear-style creature strategy; notably, combined archetypes using Badgermole Cub surpass Izzet Prowess in raw numbers according to Cards Realm analysis. **Orzhov Midrange (5.4%)** exists primarily as a prowess predator, leveraging Thoughtseize and Fatal Push alongside Preacher of the Schism.

**Izzet Phoenix (5.2%)** remains a permanent fixture, now running Cori-Steel Cutter alongside Arclight Phoenix to hedge against graveyard hate with a non-graveyard threat. **Golgari Midrange (4.8%)** is the breakout archetype of the month, featuring Ouroboroid from Lorwyn Eclipsed—one MTGO player (darg24) posted four 5-0 League finishes across four consecutive days with the deck.

Below Tier 2, Sultai Scapeshift (3.0%), Gruul Prowess (1.5%), Mono-Black Midrange (1.0%), Lotus Field Combo (0.8%), and various rogue strategies round out the field.

## No bans in February, but Cori-Steel Cutter looms large

The most recent Pioneer ban was **Heartfire Hero on November 10, 2025**, removed for making Mono-Red Aggro oppressively dominant, particularly in Best-of-One on MTG Arena. The **February 9, 2026 Banned & Restricted announcement made no changes to Pioneer**, though Wizards acknowledged that Izzet Prowess "occupies the top seat of the metagame with a strong win rate."

Wizards announced **seven B&R windows for 2026**—a dramatic increase from the previous two or three per year—signaling willingness to act more frequently. The community ban watchlist centers on several cards:

- **Cori-Steel Cutter** is the consensus #1 ban candidate. Already removed from Standard for being "a card that's a little bit stronger than everything else," it powers three separate Pioneer archetypes. Cards Realm has explicitly called it a "design mistake" in Pioneer.
- **Greasefang, Okiba Boss** draws persistent frustration for enabling turn-two kills. Some argue Parhelion II should be banned instead to preserve Greasefang as a fair card.
- **Treasure Cruise** is discussed as an enabler that exclusively benefits blue prowess/phoenix strategies, limiting archetype diversity.
- **Badgermole Cub** could become the next target if Cori-Steel Cutter is banned and creature-based decks consolidate further.

Worth noting: "Pioneer is dead" sentiment persists in some competitive circles because the format has been excluded from Pro Tours and the Magic Spotlight Series for a second consecutive year in 2026, reducing high-level competitive investment.

## TMNT arrives March 6 with full Pioneer legality

The **Magic: The Gathering | Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles** set releases on **March 6, 2026** (tabletop) and **March 3 on MTG Arena**, with prerelease events starting February 27. This is a full **320-card Universes Beyond expansion**—not a Secret Lair—and all TMT main set cards are **legal in Standard, Pioneer, and Modern**.

The four headliner Turtle cards feature artwork by original TMNT creator Kevin Eastman: Leonardo, Sewer Samurai (white, reanimation); Donatello, Mutant Mechanic (blue, artifact copying); Raphael, Ninja Destroyer (red, mass double strike); and Michelangelo, Improviser (green, cheating creatures into play). New mechanics include **Sneak** (a ninjutsu-like alternative casting cost), **Disappear** (a revolt variant), and **Mutagen tokens** (sacrifice for +1/+1 counters).

Several cards have immediate Pioneer implications:

- **Death in the Family** is a two-mana exile removal spell cited as an excellent answer to Screaming Nemesis and Greasefang—potentially shifting removal calculations across the format.
- **Donatello's Technique** functions as a one-mana Divination with Sneak, a powerful rate for blue spell-based decks.
- **Ravenous Robots** has been called "possibly the best card in the set," functioning as copies 5–8 of key artifact-aggro creatures with haste.
- **Krang, Master Mind** offers powerful artifact synergies with potential cross-format applications.

The Commander deck cards (TMC set code) are eternal-legal only. The Source Material bonus sheet (PZA) preserves each reprint's existing format legality—so Underworld Breach remains banned in Pioneer regardless of its TMNT reprint.

## Soulflayer faces a deeply hostile metagame

**Soulflayer sits at approximately 0.3% meta share with no significant competitive results since 2020.** The deck—which uses Delve to cast a 4/4 Demon cheaply while exiling keyword-rich creatures to gain flying, hexproof, indestructible, double strike, lifelink, and more—faces structural problems in the current environment.

The core issue is that **roughly 25–30% of the current metagame runs main-deck graveyard interaction or hand disruption**. Thoughtseize appears in Abzan Greasefang, Orzhov Midrange, Golgari Midrange, and Mono-Black Midrange. Graveyard Trespasser sees main-deck play in multiple black archetypes, providing incidental graveyard exile even in Game 1. Post-sideboard, Rest in Peace (from white decks comprising ~20% of the field), Unlicensed Hearse, Go Blank, and Leyline of the Void all devastate Soulflayer's gameplan.

**Matchup breakdown against the top decks:**

Soulflayer's best matchup is ironically the most-played deck: **Mono-Red Prowess struggles enormously** against a resolved Soulflayer with lifelink and hexproof, as content creator SaffronOlive noted it "felt almost impossible to lose." Linear aggro decks without interaction similarly fold to a keyword-loaded 4/4.

However, the bad matchups are numerous and meta-dominant. **Azorius Control** counters Soulflayer on the stack and has Supreme Verdict to ignore hexproof and indestructible. **Golgari and Orzhov Midrange** combine Thoughtseize with graveyard hate and efficient removal. **Greasefang variants** run Thoughtseize in Abzan builds and compete for the "graveyard deck that draws hate" slot—meaning opponents already pack graveyard answers. The critical difference is that Greasefang has backup plans (Monument to Endurance, value creatures) while **Soulflayer has virtually no Plan B** when its graveyard is disrupted.

The success of Greasefang and Izzet Phoenix as graveyard-adjacent strategies confirms that graveyard hate is present but beatable—for decks with resilience. Soulflayer's all-in nature makes it uniquely vulnerable. CardsRealm's assessment captures the consensus: Soulflayer is "a viable option for anyone looking to play at their local store... but I can't recommend the current version for a Qualifier or any other big event."

## Conclusion

Pioneer in late February 2026 is a format defined by two tensions: the Cori-Steel Cutter question and the Greasefang surge. The next B&R window will likely determine whether this metagame persists or undergoes radical restructuring. TMNT's arrival on March 6 adds 320 Pioneer-legal cards with several high-impact candidates—particularly Death in the Family as efficient exile removal that directly answers current top threats.

For Soulflayer specifically, the calculus is straightforward: this is one of the worst Pioneer metagames for the archetype. The combination of elevated graveyard hate (driven by Greasefang and Phoenix's popularity), ubiquitous Thoughtseize, and main-deck graveyard interaction creates a hostile environment. The deck retains its ability to steal games—a turn-two hexproof, indestructible, lifelink Soulflayer still beats most hands in Magic—but the consistency required for competitive play isn't there. If Cori-Steel Cutter is eventually banned and the metagame shifts away from interactive midrange toward more linear strategies, Soulflayer's positioning could improve. Until then, it remains a powerful FNM choice with polarized matchups and slim competitive viability.
